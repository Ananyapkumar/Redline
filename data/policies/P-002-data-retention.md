## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Purpose
This Policy defines how long Kestrel retains categories of records and how those records are disposed of at the end of their retention period.

### 1.2 Principle
Records shall be retained no longer than necessary to meet legal, regulatory, clinical and legitimate business requirements.

## 2. Retention Periods

### 2.1 Clinical records
Records relating to patient care shall be retained for a minimum of six (6) years from the date of creation, or from the date last in effect, whichever is later.

### 2.2 Audit and access logs
System audit logs and access logs shall be retained for a minimum of six (6) years from the date of creation.

### 2.3 Billing and claims records
Records supporting a claim submitted to a federal health care program shall be retained for a minimum of ten (10) years from the date of service.

### 2.4 Business associate agreements
Executed business associate agreements shall be retained for six (6) years following termination of the agreement.

### 2.5 Consent and authorization records
Signed patient authorizations shall be retained for six (6) years from the date the authorization expires or is revoked.

### 2.6 Employment records
Workforce records shall be retained for seven (7) years following separation.

## 3. Disposal

### 3.1 Approved methods
Electronic media containing protected health information shall be disposed of by cryptographic erasure or physical destruction. Reformatting alone is not an approved method.

### 3.2 Certificates of destruction
A certificate of destruction shall be obtained and retained for any disposal performed by a third-party vendor.

### 3.3 Litigation hold
Records subject to a litigation hold shall be preserved notwithstanding any retention period in Section 2, until the hold is released in writing by the General Counsel.

## 4. Third-Party Data

### 4.1 Subprocessor records
Retention obligations for records held by subprocessors are governed by the applicable data processing agreement.
